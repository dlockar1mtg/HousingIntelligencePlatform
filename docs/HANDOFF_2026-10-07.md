## 1. Executive Summary

The Housing Predictor began as a home-price forecasting project for two
markets important to the user: the Wichita, Kansas area and the
Dallas--Fort Worth, Texas area. It has evolved into a broader housing
decision-support system.

The current V10 platform does not merely ask whether home prices will
rise or fall. It combines market conditions, mortgage rates, local
housing supply, labor-market conditions, affordability, personal
financial readiness, Monte Carlo simulations, historical calibration,
financing scenarios, and decision thresholds to answer a more useful
question:

> **How attractive is buying a home in a given market, how ready is the
> household to buy, what conditions would improve the opportunity, and
> how does that decision fit the household's broader wealth-building
> plan?**

That makes housing a natural new domain for the Universal Investment
Platform (UIP). Housing is both a major household liability/expense
decision and a long-term wealth-building asset. The housing platform
should remain independently governed and testable in its own repository,
while publishing a governed housing decision contract into UIP.

The intended architecture is therefore:

`HousingIntelligencePlatform` → governed housing outputs →
`UniversalInvestmentPlatform`

The standalone housing repository should own data acquisition, market
modeling, validation, simulation, housing-specific decision logic, and
its own tests. UIP should consume only validated/published housing
outputs and combine them with the user's other domains: portfolio,
ETFs/stocks, crypto, metals, MTG, cash/net worth, and future purchase
planning.

## 2. Purpose

### Primary purpose

Provide a defensible, repeatable framework for deciding **when, where,
and under what financial conditions purchasing a home becomes
attractive**.

### Core questions

The system is intended to answer:

1.  What is the current housing-market regime?
2.  Are home prices in the target market likely to rise or fall over the
    next year?
3.  How strong is the current buying opportunity?
4.  Is waiting 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, or 36 months likely to improve the
    opportunity?
5.  What is the probability of reaching a better entry signal?
6.  Which target market currently offers the better opportunity?
7.  Which variables are preventing a stronger signal?
8.  What changes in mortgage rates, inventory, affordability, or
    appreciation expectations would improve the signal?
9.  Is the household financially ready to buy even if the market is
    attractive?
10. What financing structure best fits the household?
11. How should a home purchase interact with the broader net-worth and
    investment strategy?
12. Historically, have the platform's entry scores actually been
    associated with better subsequent housing outcomes?

### Current target markets

The platform currently models:

-   **Wichita Composite**
-   **DFW Composite**

The architecture is intended to support expansion to additional metros
later.

## 9. Forecast Validation

The system uses walk-forward validation rather than judging the model
only on the training sample.

A prior mature V8/V9 run produced approximately:

-   Walk-forward MAE: 0.0258
-   Directional accuracy: 98.78%
-   DFW MAE: 0.0295
-   Wichita MAE: 0.0222

The very high directional accuracy should not be treated as proof of
near-perfect forecasting. Housing prices exhibit persistence, the sample
is relatively small, and the metric may therefore look stronger than
real-world forecasting skill.

V10 also introduced a second validation question:

> Does a higher Entry Score historically correspond to better future
> housing outcomes?

This is the historical signal calibration layer.

## 10. Historical Entry Score Calibration

The calibration module groups historical entry conditions and compares
them with subsequent 12-month HPI growth.

Current diagnostics include:

-   Pearson correlation between Entry Score and future growth
-   Rank correlation
-   Mean future growth below Neutral
-   Mean future growth at Neutral-or-better
-   Decline rates
-   Calibration pass/fail

### Latest observed behavior

DFW currently passes the calibration sanity check.

Wichita currently does not.

The latest run showed approximately:

-   DFW correlation: 0.257
-   DFW rank correlation: 0.439
-   DFW calibration: PASS

-   Wichita rank correlation: 0.020

It means Wichita's current Entry Score can be used as a structured

## 11. Entry Score

The Entry Score converts many market dimensions into a single 0-100
opportunity measure.

Current component families include concepts such as:

-   Valuation
-   Supply
-   Momentum
-   Affordability
-   Economy
-   Mortgage conditions
-   Risk

The score is mapped to seven decision states.

Current signal boundaries:

     Score Signal
  -------- -------------------------
    \>= 85 Strong Buy
    \>= 72 Buy
    \>= 60 Slight Buy
    \>= 48 Neutral / Fair Value
    \>= 36 Slight Wait
    \>= 24 Wait
     \< 24 Strong Wait / High Risk

The score is not intended to mean that a person must buy whenever the
score reaches a particular level. It represents market opportunity and
is combined with personal readiness.

## 12. Monte Carlo Framework

V8 introduced probabilistic simulation.

Current configuration:

-   2,000 simulations per market
-   Six-month increments
-   Maximum horizon: 36 months

The simulation layer estimates future distributions of the Entry Score
and signal states rather than pretending there is one deterministic
future.

Outputs include:

-   Expected future Entry Score
-   Best simulated window
-   Probability of Neutral-or-better
-   Probability of Slight-Buy-or-better
-   Signal distributions by horizon

This allows the platform to answer:

> Is waiting likely to improve the opportunity, and how confident are
> we?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Meaningful Entry Window

V9 introduced a "first meaningful entry window."

The current implementation looks for meaningful improvement in:

-   Entry Score
-   Probability of Neutral-or-better
-   Probability of Slight-Buy-or-better

A known limitation is that the current rule can select a horizon because
one probability improves even if another important measure deteriorates.

Future versions should use a composite-improvement rule rather than an
"any one criterion" rule.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Sensitivity Analysis

The current V9/V10 sensitivity layer is a transparent local heuristic,
not a causal econometric estimate.

It asks how the Entry Score might respond to changes in:

-   Mortgage rates
-   Inventory growth
-   Payment-to-income ratio
-   Metro unemployment
-   Predicted appreciation

The purpose is interpretability:

> Which conditions matter most to the current recommendation?

These coefficients must not be described as causal effects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Trigger Engine

The trigger engine converts the distance to the next signal into
understandable thresholds.

For example:

-   How much would mortgage rates need to fall?
-   How much would inventory need to improve?
-   How much would affordability need to improve?
-   How much would appreciation expectations need to change?

This is useful for UIP because it can become a monitored condition
rather than a static report.

Future work should emphasize **joint trigger scenarios**, since real
housing conditions change together rather than one variable moving in
isolation.

## 18. Personal Readiness

V10 separates market attractiveness from household readiness.

This distinction is essential.

A market can be attractive while the household is not ready to purchase,
and the household can be financially ready while the market is
unattractive.

Personal readiness considers factors such as:

-   Credit
-   Down-payment reserves
-   Emergency reserves
-   Debt burden
-   Employment history
-   Goal completeness

### Important current limitation

The current V10 personal-readiness implementation is relatively simple
and should be treated as a first-generation scoring model.

The September 2026 profile mapping also exposed why personal-data
governance matters: budget categories can be double counted if debt
payments and expense categories overlap.

The corrected profile reconciles recurring monthly expenses to the
source tracker.

UIP integration should eventually eliminate duplicate manual
representations by making the UIP financial profile the authoritative
household source.

## 19. Financing Optimizer

V10 evaluates financing scenarios across:

-   15-year and 30-year terms
-   3%, 5%, 10%, and 20% down-payment scenarios

It estimates:

-   Principal and interest
-   PMI approximation
-   Property tax
-   Insurance
-   Maintenance
-   HOA
-   Back-end DTI
-   Cash required
-   Cash gap
-   Estimated total interest
-   Financing score

### Limitation

The PMI estimate is an approximation, not a lender quote.

The optimizer currently leans toward affordability/qualification logic.
Future UIP integration should optimize against the user's broader wealth
strategy rather than maximizing the amount a lender might approve.

## 25. Governance Standards

The repository should follow the same general philosophy as the other
UIP domain repositories:

### Reproducibility

A production result must be traceable to:

-   Code version / commit SHA
-   Input data dates
-   Model configuration
-   Market configuration
-   Personal-profile version where applicable
-   Run timestamp

### Fail-safe data handling

A bad or incomplete download must not silently replace a known-good
dataset.

### Explicit optionality

Unavailable optional county series should be recorded rather than
silently fabricated.

### No silent schema drift

Zillow/Realtor/Census schema changes should fail validation or create a
warning requiring review.

### Separation of concerns

Market intelligence and household financial data should remain logically
separate until the decision layer.

### No overstated certainty

Forecast confidence is not certainty.

Monte Carlo probabilities are model-derived scenario probabilities, not
guaranteed real-world frequencies.

Sensitivity coefficients currently used by the trigger system are
heuristic and must not be labeled causal.

### Calibration disclosure

Market-specific calibration status should be published with
recommendations.

### Versioned output contract

UIP should consume a stable schema with an explicit contract version.

UIP should own the **cross-domain decision layer**.

Examples:

-   How much capital should remain invested versus moved toward a home
    purchase?
-   How does buying in 2028 or 2029 affect projected net worth?
-   Should house-fund accumulation take priority over crypto/MTG/ETF
    contributions?
-   How much liquidity should be retained?
-   How does a mortgage payment affect monthly investable cash flow?
-   What is the opportunity cost of a larger down payment?
-   Should appreciated assets be liquidated for closing?
-   How does the home-purchase timeline interact with the user's
    portfolio?
-   What happens to overall asset allocation after home equity is
    introduced?

Housing Intelligence Platform should publish housing facts and
housing-domain recommendations.

UIP should decide how housing interacts with **everything else**.

## 28. Personal Financial Source of Truth

Long term, the housing repository should not maintain an independent
manually edited duplicate of household finances.

The current `personal_profile.json` is useful during standalone
operation, but UIP already has a broader goal of representing:

-   Income
-   Net worth
-   Cash
-   Investments
-   Debts
-   Contributions
-   House-purchase planning

Recommended future architecture:

`UIP personal financial state` → versioned profile contract →
`HousingIntelligencePlatform`

Housing then calculates housing-specific affordability/readiness and
returns those results to UIP.

This avoids category drift and double counting.

## 30. GitHub / Production Workflow

The new repository should move the project away from a laptop-dependent
production process.

Recommended GitHub Actions:

### CI

On pull request / push:

-   Install dependencies
-   Run unit tests
-   Run schema tests
-   Run deterministic model smoke tests
-   Validate publication schema

### Scheduled data refresh

Refresh local downloadable sources on a controlled schedule.

Requirements:

-   Validate before replacement
-   Cache or persist governed source snapshots appropriately
-   Record source dates
-   Do not commit secrets
-   Fail safely

### Forecast workflow

After validated source refresh:

-   Build model dataset
-   Run walk-forward validation
-   Train final model
-   Run Monte Carlo
-   Run decision engine
-   Run calibration
-   Build UIP contract
-   Validate UIP contract

### Publication

Only publish to UIP if:

-   Required tests pass
-   Data freshness gates pass
-   Contract schema passes
-   Required market coverage passes
-   No critical model/data-quality gate fails

The housing repo should not directly mutate arbitrary UIP internals. It
should publish through an explicit integration contract/workflow.

## 31. Secrets / Security

Do not commit:

-   Census API keys
-   Future paid-data API keys
-   Personal financial secrets
-   Credentials

Use GitHub Actions secrets/environment variables.

The personal financial profile should be treated differently from public
market data. A public repository must never contain the user's live
financial profile.

If the housing repository is public, tests should use synthetic
fixtures.

## 32. Current Known Limitations

1.  Only Wichita and DFW are configured.
2.  Wichita Entry Score historical calibration currently fails.
3.  Historical calibration is a useful sanity check, not fully
    independent causal validation.
4.  A dedicated historical replay framework for periods such as 2012,
    2018, and 2022 remains desirable.
5.  The meaningful-window rule should become composite rather than "any
    criterion improved."
6.  Trigger sensitivities are heuristic.
7.  Joint trigger scenarios are preferable to single-variable
    equivalents.
8.  Personal-readiness scoring is first-generation.
9.  Financing optimization uses approximate PMI.
10. "Comfortable purchase price" should be optimized for household
    wealth goals, not lender maximums.
11. Market ranking currently compares only configured markets.
12. Individual-property analysis does not yet exist.
13. The model should eventually distinguish market-specific feature
    importance/weights.
14. Personal finance should eventually come from UIP rather than
    duplicate JSON maintenance.
15. Production workflow should be GitHub-hosted rather than
    laptop-dependent.

## 36. Handoff Summary

The Housing Predictor has evolved into a housing intelligence and
purchase-optimization platform.

Its current strengths are:

-   Multi-source housing/economic data
-   Wichita and DFW composite markets
-   Walk-forward validation
-   Twelve-month HPI forecasting
-   Seven-state Entry Score
-   Monte Carlo future-entry modeling
-   Trigger/sensitivity analysis
-   Monitoring
-   Market ranking
-   Personal readiness
-   Financing scenarios
-   Historical signal calibration
-   Automated Zillow/Realtor/Census refresh

Its most important current caution is that Wichita's Entry Score does
not yet show strong historical calibration, even though the current
market signal has improved to Neutral / Fair Value.

The correct UIP architecture is not to absorb all housing code into the
main repository. The housing system should become its own governed
repository and publish a small, validated, versioned contract into UIP.
UIP then becomes responsible for the cross-domain decision: how housing
interacts with investments, liquidity, debt, net worth, and the
household's long-term purchase plan.

That separation preserves domain expertise, testability, provenance, and
production reliability while making housing a first-class component of
the Universal Investment Platform.
